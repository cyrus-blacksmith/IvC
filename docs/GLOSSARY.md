# Glossary of Key IvC Terms

**Resonance**: frequency-specific coupling among oscillators that yields phase alignment or phase-locked relationships across modalities.

**Coherence**: The measurable stability of phase relationships over time and scale. In IvC, coherence is not a metaphor but a quantifiable property that can be perturbed and falsified.

**$C(t)$**: Effective Coupling Strength. A time-dependent functional of the cross-modal coupling matrix $K(t)$, summarizing the aggregate interaction among modalities and hubs. Commonly defined as the matrix norm $\|K(t)\|$ or its largest eigenvalue $\lambda_{max}(K(t))$. $C(t)$ determines the system's proximity to the ignition threshold $C^*$.

**Ignition ($I$)**: The nonlinear phase transition in which weakly coupled oscillations cross a bifurcation threshold and lock into a unified, self-sustaining coherent state. Marks the operational boundary between inert chemistry and living coherence.

**Modalities**: The four fundamental physical channels through which living systems exchange and synchronize energy: bioacoustic (mechanical vibrations), bioelectric (ion flows and potentials), biophotonic (ultraweak photon emissions), and biochemical (metabolic and redox oscillations).

**Hubs**: The six classes of cellular structures where modalities converge and are transduced into coherent timing signals: DNA, cytoskeleton, mitochondria, membranes, vesicles, and regulators.

**Regulators**: Dynamic hubs that tune the strength, timing, and sensitivity of coupling among all other hubs. They adjust thresholds for activation, set gain across oscillatory networks, and stabilize coherence against fluctuations.

**$R(t)$**: Global Order Parameter. A normalized measure of system-wide coordination, derived from multimodal recordings. $R(t) \in [0,1]$ quantifies the fraction of phase-coherent energy relative to total power across modalities. Empirically, $R(t)$ may correspond to phase-locking value, spectral coherence, mutual information, or polarization-derived anisotropy metrics depending on context.

**Systemic Integrators**: Macro-scale networks (cardiorespiratory, neuro-lymphatic, circadian) that coordinate multi-organ rhythms across scales, translating cellular coherence into organismal order.

**Lattice ($L$)**: The spatial density of structural nodes or oscillators per unit volume. In biological systems, this corresponds to cytoskeletal filament density, cell packing, or network connectivity.

**Flux ($F$)**: The convergent superposition of multi-modal energy and information streams (bioacoustic, bioelectric, biophotonic, biochemical) that drives the system toward ignition.

**Hub Mass ($H$)**: The effective informational or energetic mass of integration nodes. In biological terms, this corresponds to mitochondrial density, DNA concentration, or vesicle trafficking capacity.

**Substrate Impedance ($k$)**: The substrate identity constant representing the medium's intrinsic resistance, viscosity, or elasticity. In biology, this relates to protonic conductivity of nanoconfined water, cytoplasmic viscosity, or extracellular matrix stiffness. In living systems, $k$ is minimized when water enters an ordered phase, and implicitly normalizes the system's intrinsic dissipation.

**Topological Shield ($\Psi$)**: The emergent, topologically protected coherent state that arises when a system crosses the ignition threshold. Characterized by a spectral gap in its eigenvalue distribution, the Shield renders the system robust against external perturbations and internal noise.

**Ignition Threshold**: The critical value of the dimensionless quantity $kLFH$ above which the system transitions from incoherence to the ignited state, yielding the topologically protected coherent state $\Psi$.

**Hidden Order**: Coherence that manifests not in average signals but in symmetry-sensitive measurements such as polarization variance, phase-locking value, or amplitude softening. Revealed through techniques like polarization-resolved imaging.
