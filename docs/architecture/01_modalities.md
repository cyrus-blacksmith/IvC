# 01. The Four Signaling Modalities (Section 2.1)

The IvC framework defines biological coherence as the integration of rhythms across four primary physical channels. Evidence shows each modality is rhythmic and can be entrained, serving as a dynamic network of coupled oscillators.

### 2.1.1 Bioacoustic (Mechanical)
This modality represents the mechanical oscillations and phononic signaling within the cellular and extracellular matrix.
* **Mechanisms**: Microtubules and actin filaments resonate in the MHz to GHz range, acting as piezoelectric transducers. Membranes propagate nanometer-scale surface waves, while lipid bilayers support GHz phonons. 
* **Data & Evidence**: Longitudinal waves in the cytoskeleton and soliton propagation along microtubule lattices facilitate high-speed communication. Research shows that specific frequencies (440 Hz, 14,000 Hz) can alter gene expression and suppress adipocyte differentiation by up to 15% without physical contact (Section 2.1.1).
* **Role**: Provides a structural communication channel that precedes slower biochemical diffusion.

### 2.1.2 Bioelectric (Electromagnetic)
Bioelectricity is the long-range patterning field of the organism, extending far beyond simple action potentials.
* **Mechanisms**: Spatial gradients of membrane potential ($V_{mem}$) are governed by ion channel activity and propagated through gap junction networks (Levin, 2022). 
* **Data & Evidence**: These gradients create endogenous electromagnetic fields in the ULF/ELF ranges. Pacemaker cells in the sinoatrial node bind cellular activity to organism-level coherence. 
* **Cross-modal Logic**: Ultrasound stimulation (acoustic) has been shown to reset membrane potentials (electric), proving these modalities are physically coupled.

### 2.1.3 Biophotonic (Optical)
Ultra-weak photon emissions (UPEs) are faint light signals (350–700 nm) generated by oxidative and metabolic reactions.
* **Mechanisms**: Primarily originating in mitochondria during metabolic "flashes," these photons are guided by mitochondrial networks acting as waveguides (Wang et al., 2008). 
* **Data & Evidence**: Mitochondrial redox oscillations correlate tightly with photonic pulses. Modern time-gated tomography (SPAD/SNSPD detectors) now allows single-photon resolution in living tissues (Casey et al., 2025).

### 2.1.4 Biochemical (Molecular)
Metabolic cycles are active synchronizers rather than background noise.
* **Mechanisms**: Glycolysis functions as a driven oscillator; external periodic forcing induces phase-locking and reproducible phase-response curves (Hess & Boiteux, 1971).
* **Integration**: Metabolism modulates mitochondrial photon emissions through redox-linked "mitoflashes," while ATP rhythms synchronize the timing of vesicle release.
