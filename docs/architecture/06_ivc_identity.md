### 2.7 Framework Synthesis and Testability
The framework is testable. It predicts that targeted perturbations in one modality should produce measurable coherence shifts in others. Specifically:
* Photon conduction along microtubules, if disrupted, should alter mitochondrial and electrical rhythms (Jibu et al., 1994; Hameroff & Penrose, 2014).
* Membrane voltage resets during regeneration should phase-shift vesicle release and cytoskeletal dynamics (Levin, 2022; Durant et al., 2023).
* Acoustic entrainment with ultrasound should re-time metabolic oscillations and extracellular vesicle release (Tyler, 2017; Guo et al., 2023).
* Metabolic forcing through glycolytic oscillators should induce phase-locked changes in redox state, ion channel conductance, and photon emission (Hess & Boiteux, 1971; Wolf et al., 2000; Casey et al., 2025).

### 2.8 Closing
The framework is both defined and adaptable. Its elegance lies in the 4+6 core, where four modalities converge through six hubs to generate coherence across scales. Yet its structure is resilient: new hubs, substrates, or auxiliary modalities can be incorporated as evidence grows, guided by transparent promotion criteria. Regulators and integrators add an adaptive tier that tunes and stabilizes coherence without disrupting the core architecture. This adaptability ensures that IvC remains grounded in evidence while open to discovery, positioning resonance not as a metaphor, but as life's measurable and testable architecture.
